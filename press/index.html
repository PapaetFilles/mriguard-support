In one sentence

MRI Guard turns the magnetometer in an iPhone or Apple Watch into a personal alarm for the one hazard every MRI department knows and still gets caught by: a phone or watch carried too close to the scanner.

50 words

Near an MRI magnet a phone or watch is wiped, damaged or pulled from the hand in a fraction of a second. MRI Guard measures the magnetic field around the device and sounds a loud alarm as it climbs toward the danger zone — on the iPhone from the pocket, on the Apple Watch all shift long.

150 words

MRI Guard is a magnetic-field alarm for people who work around MRI scanners: radiographers and technologists, radiologists, nurses, physicists and service engineers. The app reads the magnetometer built into every iPhone and Apple Watch and sounds a loud, unmistakable alarm — as a critical alert, through Silent and Focus — when the field crosses a threshold. The default is 3 mT, the EU action level for attraction and projectile risk (Directive 2013/35/EU); it is adjustable from 0.1 to 5 mT.

On the iPhone, monitoring starts by itself on arrival at a saved workplace and keeps measuring from the pocket. On the Apple Watch, an MRI shift is a real workout: the field is measured on the wrist for the whole shift, screen on or off, and the shift is saved to Health with heart rate, distance, energy and a log of every approach to the magnet.

MRI Guard is built by an MR physicist and is deliberately honest about its limits: an awareness aid, not a certified safety device. Available in ten languages. One purchase, no subscription.

The story

Twenty years beside MRI magnets, dozens of patents, the German Future Prize — and still the same accident every year in every department: a colleague's phone or watch pulled into the scanner. Apple put a magnetometer into every iPhone and Watch; nobody had turned it into a warning. MRI Guard is one physicist's answer, measured on his own scanner and worn on his own wrist.

Why it matters — three facts to quote

  • An MRI magnet is never off. Its field reaches metres beyond the scanner, and it pulls on steel with a force that grows with the cube of proximity — the last half metre is where a phone leaves the hand.
  • The EU sets 3 mT as the action level for attraction and projectile risk near strong magnets (Directive 2013/35/EU). Every iPhone and Apple Watch carries a sensor that can read that field — nobody had wired it to an alarm.
  • Hospital MRI shifts are walking-heavy — six to ten kilometres a day in the literature — in loud, windowless rooms. That is why the Watch records the shift as a workout with the field log inside it.
